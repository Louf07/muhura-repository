Malaria is a life-threatening, but preventable and curable, disease caused by parasites of the genus Plasmodium. It is primarily transmitted to humans through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. +1 🦟 Transmission and Causes The disease is caused by five parasite species that infect humans: P. falciparum: The deadliest and most prevalent, particularly in Africa. P. vivax: The dominant species outside of sub-Saharan Africa; it can lie dormant in the liver and cause relapses months or years later. P. ovale & P. malariae: Less common, with P. ovale also capable of liver dormancy. P. knowlesi: Primarily found in Southeast Asia, it spreads from monkeys to humans (zoonotic). The Transmission Cycle: The Bite: An infected mosquito injects sporozoites into the bloodstream. Liver Stage: Parasites travel to the liver, where they multiply. Blood Stage: They enter red blood cells, multiply again, and cause the cells to burst. This stage is when symptoms occur. +1 Next Host: A new mosquito bites the infected person, ingesting the parasite and continuing the cycle. 🤒 Symptoms Symptoms typically appear 10–15 days after the bite. Common (Mild) Symptoms Fever and shivering chills (the "malaria attack" often occurs in cycles). Headache, muscle aches, and fatigue. Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Severe Symptoms (Medical Emergency) Cerebral Malaria: Confusion, seizures, or coma caused by parasites blocking blood vessels to the brain. Respiratory Distress: Difficulty breathing due to fluid in the lungs or metabolic issues. Organ Failure: Kidney failure, liver damage, or a ruptured spleen. Severe Anemia: Massive destruction of red blood cells. 🛡️ Prevention and Protection Prevention focuses on "Vector Control" (avoiding mosquitoes) and "Chemoprophylaxis" (preventive medicine). Insecticide-Treated Nets (ITNs): Sleeping under bed nets is the most effective way to prevent bites. Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS): Spraying the walls of homes with insecticide. Repellents & Clothing: Using DEET-based repellents and wearing long sleeves/pants after dusk. Vaccines: As of 2026, two major vaccines—RTS,S (Mosquirix) and R21/Matrix-M—are being rolled out across Africa, significantly reducing severe malaria in children. 💊 Treatment and Diagnosis Diagnosis is performed via a rapid diagnostic test (RDT) or by examining a blood smear under a microscope. ACTs (Artemisinin-based Combination Therapies): The gold standard for treating uncomplicated P. falciparum malaria. Emergency Care: Severe malaria requires intravenous (IV) medication and hospital monitoring. Resistance: A growing concern in 2026 is the increasing resistance of parasites to artemisinin in Southeast Asia and parts of Africa. 📊 Global Impact (2024-2026 Trends) Cases: In 2024, there were an estimated 282 million cases and 610,000 deaths. Demographics: Children under 5 remain the most vulnerable, accounting for roughly 75% of all malaria deaths in the African Region. Geography: The WHO African Region carries a disproportionate burden, accounting for about 95% of all cases and deaths globally. [!IMPORTANT] If you have recently traveled to a tropical region and develop a fever, seek medical attention immediately. Malaria can progress from "flu-like" to fatal within 24 hours if untreated.
